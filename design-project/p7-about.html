About BikeNest

Every good parking spot, known by someone who already rides past it.

BikeNest is a community-maintained map of bicycle parking. Cyclists add the racks, stations and lockers they use, confirm details when they change, and flag places that disappear — so the next rider searches once, and arrives knowing the details were checked by someone on the same route.

How it works

A loop that runs on riders

The map stays accurate because the people who use it maintain it. Three roles, one loop:

1

Search

Enter a destination or use your location once. BikeNest shows parking near where you're going, with type, cost, security and — crucially — how recently each detail was confirmed.

2

Choose with confidence

Freshness badges and verification counts tell you what's current and what's worth double-checking. You always see when information was last verified — never a false sense of certainty.

3

Give back

Parked somewhere? Tap "I parked here". Found a new rack? Add it in under a minute. Noticed something wrong? Report it. Every signal keeps the map honest.

The system behind the badges

How verification & freshness work

Every parking location carries a last-verified timestamp, updated whenever a rider confirms its details — through "I parked here", by confirming a security feature, or by uploading a current photo. Older information is visibly marked as stale instead of silently presented as current.

Verification signals are aggregated: you see how many riders confirmed something, never who. Individual parking histories are private and kept only for a short period — a verification is a signal about the location, not a record about you.

Freshness also feeds recommendations: between two otherwise similar spots, the one verified more recently ranks first.

Default freshness categories

  • Fresh verified < 30 days ago
  • Recently 30–90 days
  • Aging 90–180 days
  • Stale 180–365 days — check before relying on it
  • Very stale > 365 days — treated as unconfirmed

These thresholds are product defaults for reading older data — not claims about factual validity. A "Stale" spot may be perfectly fine; it just deserves an extra look.

Contributing

Four ways to keep the map alive

All contributions need a free account. Everything you add is reviewed before it goes public, and credited to "a community contributor" — never your name.

Add a location

Rack, parking station, locker — drop a pin, pick a type, add cost and opening hours if you know them.

Verify

Confirm what you can see — "I parked here", or a check on details like staffing, cover or CCTV.

Review

Tell other riders what a spot is actually like at 7 am, in the rain, with a cargo bike.

Photograph

A clear photo is the fastest way to trust a spot. Location data is stripped from uploads.

Community care

How moderation works

Open contribution needs guardrails. A small moderation team reviews activity and keeps the map trustworthy — with rules that apply to them too.

Reviewed, not pre-censored

Reports and proposed changes are reviewed by moderators; inappropriate reviews and photos are hidden, and locations that no longer exist are invalidated.

An audited trail

Every moderation action creates an audit event. Moderators cannot silently alter the record of what was done and when.

A right of reply

If your contribution is hidden or your account restricted, you're told why and can contest the decision — a person reviews it, and content is restored when a call was wrong.

Abuse prevention

Rate limits and abuse protection apply to accounts and reports alike, so the report feature can't be weaponized to hide good parking.
